# OWL-613 · Refresh a rebase overlap whose colleague's claim was deleted before the base moved

Settles: [build plan](../build-plan.md), "P10 · Teams", "Other runners' tickets", "Built (OWL-587)", the *Overlaps* bullet and the first of its *Accepted residuals*.

## The decision (2026-11-22)

The ticket asked to decide from the P10 exit gate's counted collisions whether to close the gap. There were none to read, for two reasons:

- The gate had not run. OWL-591 showed Done because the body of pull request #592, the 0.2.0 release it runs on, named it ("does not close OWL-591"), and Linear closed it at that merge on 2026-11-21 at 10:41 UTC, before the 72-hour run started. Nothing in the records or the build plan held a count.
- The gate could not count this residual even once run. Its project (`owlshift-team-gate`) sets no `policy.overlap`, so it runs `exclusive`: a colleague's claim on a shared file holds the Build, and no overlap is ever recorded. It sets no `[forge] merge` either. Its collisions answer the other residual of "Other runners' tickets", two admissions within the same minute.

So the decision was made with the maintainer from the code. It was to close the gap:

- The miss was not a race between two watches that both run. The holder deletes its claim at the pass after the one that reads its merge (the sweep reads the snapshot taken at the start of the pass), about one to two minutes after the merge. A runner that passes every minute reads the moved base before that. The miss came from the runner that does not pass in that window: its watch stopped, its machine asleep (P7's gate saw a lid closed for nine hours), its forge reads paused. It also came from a refresh not taken at that one pass, since the refresh is read again at each pass and the overlap counted no more once the claim was gone. With runners on laptops this is the ordinary case.
- Under the default `[forge] merge = never`, a refresh missed on a change that merges cleanly is lost, not late: the person sees checks that ran on the older base. Only a textual conflict brought it back, through the rebase run the forge's conflict starts. Under merge on green the merge's own read of the base (OWL-520) refreshes the delivery's paths before the merge, so there it was late.

Rejected: keeping in watch's memory the colleague tickets seen claimed, which a watch restart or an upgrade loses, both common triggers; reading the state of the colleague's pull request (`owlshift/<ticket>`) on the forge for each such sibling, one read per sibling and pass to tell a merge from a close; a record without code.

## As built (2026-11-22)

- `held::sharings_of` and `held::overlaps_of` take the project's `Claims` switch in place of the list of claimed tickets. A sibling with no ref here is named only by the ticket's own record. It counts under `remote`, as another runner's ticket, whether its claim still stands or not, and never under `local`, where it is a ticket forgotten here. The list of claims added nothing beyond the switch: claims are read only under `remote`.
- Every reader of a ticket's overlaps goes through it and reads the same rule: watch's refresh (`watch/refresh.rs`, the switch from `memory.claimed.remote`), and through `OnDemand::sharings` (the switch from `Prepared::claims`) a command's or a repair's read of the base (`overlap_base`), the review after a resolution, a resume's rebase (`resume_reach`) and the Build's brief, which lists such a sibling as it lists a merged sibling of this runner's. `ClaimedMemory::tickets`, left without a caller, is gone.
- Tests: `held::tests::overlaps_are_read_from_both_sides_while_the_sibling_counts` (the sibling with no ref counted under `remote`, not under `local`); the bench scenarios `overlap_scenarios::a_colleagues_merge_refreshes_the_pull_request_once_its_claim_is_gone` (the claim fetched, the change merged, the claim deleted, then one rebase run of cause `overlap` onto the merge) and `under_local_claims_a_sibling_without_a_ref_refreshes_nothing`. The first scenario fails with the earlier rule.

## Residuals

- Under `remote`, a colleague's ticket closed unmerged or released, or a ticket of this runner's forgotten, that the ticket's own record names keeps counting. Each base move that changes a path they share, and that the head lacks, costs a refresh rebase run (37 minutes median on Locary, the rebase runs measured in [architecture](../architecture.md) section 5, "A stopped holder yields"). A sibling of this runner's closed unmerged counts no more. Telling the two apart needs the forge read rejected above.
- An overlap only the colleague's runner recorded, because its ticket was admitted second, is not known here. A claim publishes what its ticket holds, not its overlaps, so that refresh is missed whatever the claim. Publishing them changes the claim's format; it is left to a follow-up.
