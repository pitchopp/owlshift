# OWL-622 · File a repair ticket when the base breaks, admitted by the runner and dispatched first

Settles: [architecture](../architecture.md) section 2, rule 6, and section 5, "A broken base", "The repair ticket"; [build plan](../build-plan.md), "P6 · Parallel", part 16.

## As built (2026-11-22)

- *The switch.* `policy.base_repair` (`Policy::base_repair`, `true` when absent), read at the base the step fetched (`project::base_repair_at`), as `policy.overlap` is.
- *The record.* `base.json` is format 2: a checked entry keeps `repair`, `unfiled` or `filed` with the ticket and the SHA-256 of its title and description as the tracker answered them once filed (`owlshift_core::base::Repair`, `KeptRepair`). Format 1 reads with none and refuses a `repair`. The repair sits on the entry beside its hold, so a lift keeps it: the runner admits a repair only while an entry naming it holds (`Checks::repairs`, `Checks::held_for`), and a hold that opens again, or another on the same step and command, takes an open one again (`Checks::reusable`).
- *The filing* (`OnDemand::file_repair`, `on_demand/base_hold.rs`). Called where a hold opens, a match in `settle` or a check's `Opened` in `run_check`, and by a held step whose hold was never tried (its first try found the project's lock taken). One section of the project's lock holds the whole filing, tracker calls included, so two steps never file twice. In order: an open repair of `Checks::reusable` (the tracker's state type not completed, canceled or duplicate), then one found by `Tracker::proposed` on the deterministic title and the description's marker line (`[owlshift] REPAIR · <commit> · <step> <command hash>`, which also finds a colleague's runner's), then `Tracker::file_repair`. The assignee is the `takes = "mine"` operator, else the tracker key's account when no app's, else the held ticket's assignee; none is `repair_unfiled`. A refusal is `repair_unfiled`, kept as `unfiled`, and not tried again until a hold opens again. An open ticket taken again whose text the tracker cannot read now, nor `base.json` kept, is `repair_unfiled` too but not kept, so the hold's next step tries again (found in review: an empty hash would never admit it; `a_repair_found_but_unread_is_tried_again`). A filing is a project `decision` event (`base` = `repair`, `ticket`, `commit`, `step`, `command`, `reused`); a problem after filing is `repair_problem`.
- *The ticket.* Title "Repair the base: `<command>` fails at `<commit>`", the command on one line and cut at 80 characters, backticks made quotes (`base::repair_title`). The description (`base::repair_description`): the step and command, the reason, the last 2 KiB of the output in a fence one backtick longer than any run in it (`base::fenced`, so no mention, link or Markdown of it renders), the commits since the last passing verdict of the same commands when `base.json` keeps one (at most 20), the re-check, the person's step for a fix outside the repository, and the marker line.
- *The adapters.* `Tracker::file_repair` with `Repair { held, title, description, ready, label, assignee }`. Linear: the held issue's team and project, the team's state named exactly as the ready state, `priority: 1`, the admission label by name (the team's, else the workspace's; `label_named` shared with the proposals' label), then the assignment as a proposal's (OWL-625, `assign` shared). GitHub Issues: the ready stage's label, the admission label and the most urgent label `[tracker.priorities]` names, the assignee's login read from its numeric id (`assignee_login`, shared with `open_proposal`). Markdown: a folder after the held ticket's key, stage ready, priority urgent, the label and the assignee, a Markdown ticket's decider (`file_after`, shared with `propose`). No relation is written.
- *Admitted by the runner.* `OnDemand::runner_gesture`: a ticket named by a holding entry whose current title and description still hash to the filed text gets the gesture `owlshift` (`base::RUNNER_GESTURE`, the project's gesture kind and name) in `drain_checks` (before any admitter or `takes` read) and in `gesture_for`; `run_intake` lets that account through for it. Edited since filing, it needs a person's gesture as any ticket. The drain reads the repairs once a pass (`Row::repair`): its intake and its row come first, and the core's `Ticket::repair` dispatches it before the turn's order with `max_in_flight` lifted (`schedule::repair_caps`); the open pull requests' cap and the machine's runs still apply.
- *First, and not held.* The capacity is lifted in the dispatch's admission (`OnDemand::admit`), a person's `continue` (`admit_stage`) and watch's turn (`Memory::repairs`, added to the pass's holders, to a holder taking the turn after a step, and to the answer check's RESUME). `before_base` lets the repair's Build go for every hold naming it (`OnDemand::repair_of`, its wait file dropped), and `base_broken` takes its failure as its branch's. Its Build's admission, a person's `continue` of it and its delivery's check (`delivery_collision`, found on the bench: the repair, verified, was held back for a held ticket's zone) leave out each Owlshift holder not delivered whose branch's merge-base with the base is a held commit, or cannot be read (`leave_base_held`): weighed as yielded under `rebase` (OWL-621's record, on the repair's side alone), left out under `exclusive`. Watch predicts no collision for a repair: its step's admission decides.
- *A broken setup* (the maintainer's decision of 2026-11-22). `RunSpec::repairs_setup`, filled for the repair's Build-role runs from the hold naming it when its step is `setup`: `SetupRun::repairs` lets the harness start, told (`setup_failure`), when the setup fails at exactly that command, the command's own fault. Every other run gets `None`.
- *Lifted first.* A check's `Lifted` in `run_check` on an entry whose repair was filed posts a LIFTED comment on it (`MarkerKind::Lifted`, `base::lifted_comment`) unless the tracker shows it completed or canceled; a post that fails is `repair_not_told`.
- *Tests.* Core: `a_holds_repair_is_kept_and_offered_again_on_the_same_command`, `a_repair_goes_first_whatever_the_capacity_counts`. Contracts: `a_holds_repair_survives_its_file_and_format_1_reads_without_one`, `the_base_repair_is_a_policy_key_on_by_default`. Adapters: `a_repair_goes_to_the_ready_state_urgent_labelled_and_assigned` and `a_repair_that_cannot_be_filed_as_designed_is_refused_unwritten` (Linear), `a_repair_is_an_issue_ready_admitted_urgent_and_assigned` (GitHub), `a_repair_is_filed_ready_urgent_labelled_and_assigned` (Markdown). Runner: `a_repair_starts_through_its_own_setup_command_only`, `a_repairs_text_quotes_the_output_and_is_admitted_unchanged`. Bench (`repair_scenarios.rs`), one `watch --drain` under `max_in_flight = 2`: DEMO-1's done Build held at its gate's setup, the repair filed once, DEMO-2's Build held by the same hold before its run, the repair taken in by the runner's gesture and dispatched past the capacity the two held Builds take, its intake's zone the one DEMO-1 holds, started through its setup failure, delivered and merged; `main`'s head checked and passing, DEMO-1 rebased onto it and regated, DEMO-2, with no commit of its own, moved onto it at its step into Build (OWL-274) and built there. Then the switch off, a refusing tracker, and a lift told on the repair. `child_steps.rs`'s base-wait test and `base_scenarios.rs`'s busy-drain test set `base_repair = false`: a repair would take the turns they count, and the second writes a ticket under the identifier the repair took.

## Live check R1 (2026-11-22)

Linear's schema, read-only, through the project's key: `query { __type(name: "IssueCreateInput") { inputFields { name description type { name } } } }`. It lists `priority: Int` ("0 = No priority, 1 = Urgent, 2 = High, 3 = Medium, 4 = Low"), `assigneeId`, `stateId`, `labelIds`, `teamId` and `projectId`. It settles the Linear filing's `priority: 1` beside the fields `propose` already sends; no issue was created, the workspace being at its free plan's cap. The GitHub Issues filing reuses the calls OWL-536 and OWL-625 checked live (`POST /repos/{owner}/{repo}/issues` with `labels` and `assignees`, `GET user/{id}`).

## Built otherwise than designed

- *A lift `owlshift gate` records* is not told on the repair: that command reads no tracker (written into the architecture's "Its end").
- *A repair outside the repository* takes the person's step path built in OWL-619 (a `person_step` question of its Build); nothing was added for it.
- *"Both are rebased"*, the acceptance's words: a held Build with commits of its own is rebased onto the merge (OWL-427), one held before any run of its own is moved there by its next step into Build (OWL-274), as the design already says; the bench plays one of each, since a Build configured at a held commit is held before it starts.
- *The held tickets' side of the overlap* is not recorded: under `rebase` the repair's record takes what it shares with them, as a yielded holder's; they go on by OWL-427's rebase or the step's fast-forward once the repair merged.

## Accepted residuals

- A crash between the tracker's answer and `base.json`'s write leaves the hold untried, so its next held step searches before filing: the marker finds the first ticket, unless the tracker cannot search (GitHub Issues searches only its proposals' label), where a second one is filed.
- A repair merged whose head still fails opens no hold until a ticket's failure matches the new head; that hold's repair is then a new ticket, the merged one being completed.
- An Owlshift older than this one reads a format-2 `base.json` as an error, which holds nothing (`base_unchecked`) until it is upgraded.
- While a repair runs, `owlshift status` and `owlshift doctor` count it among the tickets in flight, one past `max_in_flight`.
- The repair's fix and recovery runs also start through the failed setup, each a harness run, when its fix is outside the repository and its Build has not asked the person's step.
