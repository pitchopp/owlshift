# OWL-636 · Publish a ticket's overlaps in its claim so a colleague's runner refreshes for them

Settles: [build plan](../build-plan.md), "P10 · Teams", "Other runners' tickets", "Built (OWL-587)", the *Overlaps* bullet and its *Accepted residuals*; the second residual of [OWL-613's record](OWL-613.md).

## The decision (2026-11-22)

Built otherwise than the title says: no claim publishes overlaps. At the ticket's alignment on 2026-11-22 the maintainer chose design A, where each runner finds its own tickets' overlaps in the other runners' claims.

- The ticket asked first whether a simpler design removes the class. The overlap a colleague's runner records lives in its ticket ref, which never leaves its machine (OWL-122). Its claim already publishes what its ticket holds (`held`), though, and the rule that found the overlap, `collision::weigh` under `rebase`, tells a ticket its overlaps with every other Owlshift ticket, before or after it. This runner finds the same overlap from that claim, as a widening already weighs colleagues' claims.
- Whatever the design, the record is written on this side: the colleague's claim is deleted after its merge, and since OWL-613 only the ticket's own record keeps that sibling counting.

Rejected:

- Publishing the overlaps in the claim (format 3), the ticket's proposal. The reading runner needs the same write. An older runner reads a format-3 claim as one it cannot read, which holds nothing it can name, so a team on mixed versions loses its collisions with that colleague until both upgrade. And it helps only when the colleague's runner writes the new format.
- Refreshing on the ticket's own held set under `remote`, which needs no record. Every base change inside its zones, people's merges included, would cost a rebase run (37 minutes median on Locary), and the repairs' bound parks a ticket past six.

## As built (2026-11-22)

- `watch/claimed.rs`, `Watch::record_claims_overlaps`: called at each pass once the stopped holders yielded, before the collisions and the refreshes read the refs, under `rebase` and remote claims only. Other runners' tickets come from their readable claims through `in_flight::Outside::holders`, with no pull request and no person's ticket: those are never an overlap's sibling and could only hold the ticket back.
- `claims_overlaps` weighs each ticket ref that holds (`held::holds`), keeps a `held.json` and has not yielded, at its own claim's time when it has one (`held::by_claim`), as watch's collisions order it. Weighed at its dispatch time, a ticket not delivered could see a later colleague as one that goes before it, and be held back with no overlap told.
- An overlap the record lacks is found on a copy first, so a pass with nothing new takes no lock. It is then written as a yield is (`write_yield`): the project's and the ticket's lock while the ref is the one the snapshot read, the record read and decided again, written through the run file, one `decision` event `overlap` = `recorded`, `by` = `claim` per sibling, and the ref read again in the pass. A ticket whose step's child runs, a busy project or a moved ref gets nothing that pass, and no line; a failed write is one `watch_unreadable` warning.
- `architecture.md` is unchanged. Its "recorded once, by the ticket whose admission or widening found it, and read from both sides" (section 5, "Tolerated overlap", "Readings settled") speaks of one runner's tickets, whose refs it reads; the rules for colleagues' overlaps are the build plan's "Other runners' tickets", where OWL-587 and OWL-613 wrote theirs.
- Tests: `watch::claimed::tests::a_ticket_here_overlaps_a_colleagues_claim_while_it_holds` (a followed ticket's overlap; none once yielded or back at Ready; a ticket not delivered told it at its claim's time, held back at its dispatch time); the bench scenario `overlap_scenarios::a_colleagues_later_overlap_is_recorded_here_and_refreshes_at_its_merge` (the claim read through watch's own fetch, the overlap recorded once over six passes, then the change merged and the claim deleted and pruned, and one rebase run of cause `overlap` onto the merge). The scenario fails with the pass's call removed.

## Residuals

- A colleague's ticket whose claim no watch here read while it published what it held, this machine asleep from that ticket's admission to its merge, is not known here. Publishing the overlaps in the claim would miss it the same way.
- Recording from claims widens OWL-613's first residual: a colleague's ticket recorded from its claim alone, then closed unmerged or released, keeps refreshing the ticket each time the base changes the paths they share. An expired claim is recorded too: it still holds what it names (build plan, "Claims", *Expired*).
- A record that keeps `MAX_OVERLAPS` (100) siblings records no new one from a claim, and gives no `overlap_unrecorded` warning where a widening gives one: watch would give it at every pass.
